# Checkpoint 1 — Choose Your Person and Topic

**Focus:** Advocacy and Action — Listening to Someone's Experience
**Proficiency Target:** Advocacy and Action L3

---

## What You Are Doing

In this checkpoint, you will do two things.

First, you will choose one person in your life to talk to. This person has a challenge or a problem in their life.

Second, you will have a short conversation with that person. You will use a conversation guide to help you ask good questions and write down what they say.

You are not doing a formal interview. This is just a real conversation with someone you know.

---

## Class Days for This Checkpoint: Days 1–2

---

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Part A — Choose Your Person

**Step 1.** Read the list of examples below.

These are examples of people you could talk to:

| Person | Example Challenge They Might Have |
|---|---|
| A parent or guardian | Something hard about their job or their health |
| A grandparent | Something that has gotten more difficult as they have gotten older |
| A neighbor | Something in the neighborhood they wish was different |
| A family friend | A problem in their work or community |
| A sibling or cousin | Something at school or in their daily life |

**Step 2.** Think about one real person you know.

Ask yourself: Is there someone in my life who has a problem or a challenge I have noticed?

**Step 3.** Write the person's name or relationship here:

> I am going to talk to: ___________________________________________
> This person is my: ___________________________________________

**Here is an example:**

> I am going to talk to: my uncle Marco
> This person is my: uncle

---

### Part B — Get Ready for the Conversation

Before you talk to your person, you need to know what to ask.

**Step 4.** Read the conversation guide below.

You do not have to say these words exactly. They are just to help you get started.

---

**Conversation Guide**

Say this to open the conversation:

> "I'm working on a school project about real challenges people face. Can I ask you a few questions about something that's hard for you?"

Then ask one or two of these questions:

> "What is something in your life that feels difficult or frustrating right now?"
>
> "Is there something you wish was better — at work, at home, or in your neighborhood?"
>
> "Is there a problem that you think about a lot?"

Then ask a follow-up question to learn more:

> "Can you tell me a little more about that?"
>
> "Why does that make things difficult?"
>
> "Who else does this affect?"

Close the conversation like this:

> "Thank you. I'm going to do some research on this and make something in Canva about it. I'll show you what I find."

---

**Step 5.** Have the conversation with your person.

Write down notes while you are talking, or right after.

---

### Part C — Write Down What You Learned

**Step 6.** Fill in this frame using what your person told you.

> The person I talked to is: ___________________________________________
>
> The challenge they described is: ___________________________________________
>
> One reason this challenge is hard for them is: ___________________________________________
>
> One thing I want to learn more about is: ___________________________________________

**Here is an example of a completed frame:**

> The person I talked to is: my uncle Marco
>
> The challenge they described is: he was recently diagnosed with high blood pressure and doesn't understand what that means or what to do about it
>
> One reason this challenge is hard for them is: his doctor gives him a lot of information at appointments and he can't remember it all
>
> One thing I want to learn more about is: what causes high blood pressure and what can people do to manage it

---

### Part D — Confirm Your Topic

Before you finish Checkpoint 1, you need to confirm your topic with your teacher.

**Step 7.** Show your teacher what you wrote in Part C.

**Step 8.** Talk to your teacher about whether this topic works for research and for Canva.

**Step 9.** Fill in the final confirmation below:

> My confirmed topic is: ___________________________________________
>
> My teacher confirmed this on: ___________________________________________

---

## You Are Done When:

- [ ] You have written your person's name or relationship in Step 3.
- [ ] You have had the conversation with your person.
- [ ] You have filled in all four blanks in the Part C frame.
- [ ] Your teacher has confirmed your topic.

---

## Proficiency Check — Advocacy and Action L3

Your teacher will use these things to check your work:

1. You used sentence stems to summarize key points from your conversation with your person.
2. You identified a specific challenge that a real person faces.
3. You described how the challenge makes things hard for that person, using at least one specific detail.

If all four blanks in Part C are filled in with specific information (not just "they have a problem"), you are at L3.
