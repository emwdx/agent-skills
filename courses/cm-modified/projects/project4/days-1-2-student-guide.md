## Conversation Guide

**Cut this out or keep it on your phone. Use it during your conversation.**

---

**To open the conversation, say:**

> "I'm doing a school project about real challenges people face. Can I ask you a few questions?"

---

**Then ask one or two of these questions:**

> "What is something in your life that feels difficult or frustrating right now?"

> "Is there something you wish was better — at work, at home, or in your neighborhood?"

> "Is there a problem you think about a lot?"

---

**Then ask one follow-up question:**

> "Can you tell me a little more about that?"

> "Why does that make things difficult?"

> "Who else does this affect?"

---

**To close the conversation, say:**

> "Thank you. I'm going to do some research on this and make something in Canva about it. I'll show you what I find."

**Here is an example of a completed summary frame:**

> The person I talked to is: my uncle Marco
>
> The challenge they described is: he was recently diagnosed with high blood pressure and doesn't really understand what it means or what to do about it
>
> One reason this challenge is hard for them is: his doctor gives him a lot of information at appointments and he can't remember all of it
>
> One thing I want to learn more about is: what causes high blood pressure and what can people do to manage it
