## Project description
This is an overview of another student's assignment for a different topic of waste management. This could be architects designing buildings to be safer, designers of warning systems,administrators coming up with safety plans, and people that want to be prepared for earthquakes when they happen.


Tab 1: Overview
Project Title: Waste Systems Audit & Prototype
This document outlines your unique path for the Changemakers final project. We have adapted the project to a solo, research-driven track that allows you to work at your own pace and show your proficiency in design and systems thinking.
While the rest of the class is working in groups and scheduling live interviews, you will focus on a "Digital Action Plan". You are not expected to find an outside stakeholder for a live interview; instead, you will become an expert through a deep audit of an existing organization.
The goal is to use research and AI tools to build a digital prototype—like a website or a tracker—that addresses a real-world problem in the trash and recycling sector.

Tab 2: Checkpoint 1 - Deep Research Audit
Focus: Adaptive Thinking & Stakeholder Perspectives
Instead of conducting a face-to-face interview, you will perform a "Knowledge Audit" on a specific organization (e.g., a local recycling center, an ocean cleanup NGO, or a waste management company).
Identify Your Subject: Choose one organization working on trash or recycling.
Mission Analysis: Analyze their mission statement and public reports to identify their core priorities.
Knowledge Audit: Use a chart to record "What we know" vs. "What we would like to know" based on their public data.
The Problem from Their View: In one paragraph, describe the specific perspective this organization has on why waste is a problem in their community.
Assessment Goal: Show that you can summarize key ideas from multiple sources.

Tab 3: Checkpoint 2 - Systems Analysis
Focus: Root Cause & Impact
Now that you understand the organization, you need to dive into the problem they are trying to solve. Use these systems thinking tools to map out the issue:
Macro, Meso, and Micro Levels: Describe the waste problem from a large-scale system level (laws), a community level (local systems), and an individual level (everyday experience).
The Five Whys: Pick one core issue (e.g., "People don't recycle correctly") and ask "Why?" five times to find the deepest root cause.
Root Cause Categories: Identify at least one Physical cause (e.g., broken equipment), one Human cause (e.g., lack of knowledge), and one Organizational cause (e.g., faulty policy) for your problem.
Assessment Goal: Use systems thinking tools to identify patterns and contributing factors.

Tab 4: Checkpoint 3 - AI Prototype
Focus: Creativity & Design Thinking
You will use Gemini and Canvas to build a digital tool that addresses the problem you researched.
The Prompt: Write a prompt for Gemini that answers three questions:
What does the tool do? (e.g., "An app that helps users identify what is recyclable.")
What does the screen look like?
What happens when you use it?
Technical Requirement: Add this line to your prompt: "Please write code in the Canvas that I can use to try out the app. Use local storage, not firebase".
Iteration Log: Record at least two changes you asked the AI to make to improve the tool.
Assessment Goal: Demonstrate iteration on a product using teacher-directed tools.

Tab 5: Checkpoint 4 - Resilience & Implementation
Focus: Action & Advocacy
Think about how this tool would work if it were a real-world project.
SMART Goals: Create a Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound goal for your project's potential impact.
Minimum Viable Project (MVP): Describe the simplest possible version of your project that could still create a positive change.
Plan for Resilience: Identify one thing that could go wrong with your prototype and write a plan for how you would fix it.
Assessment Goal: Propose an action plan based on researched stakeholder needs.

Tab 6: Checkpoint 5 - Peer Feedback & Reflection
Focus: Collaboration
Even though you are working solo, you are still part of the class community. This checkpoint is about your presence in the room.
Gallery Walk: During our class showcase, visit the screens of at least two other students.
Peer Feedback: Provide written feedback on one peer’s project, focusing on one design decision they made that was different from yours.
Self-Reflection: Write a short reflection on your personal goals for this project and whether you met your own deadlines.
Assessment Goal: Identify next steps and personal goals while giving feedback to others.
