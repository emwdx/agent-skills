## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Part A — Three Levels of the Problem

There are three levels to any big problem.
Here is what each level means:

| Level | What It Means | Example (Earthquakes) |
|---|---|---|
| **Macro** | The big picture — laws, government, global systems | No national law requires old buildings to be retrofitted |
| **Meso** | The community level — local systems, schools, hospitals | A school district has no earthquake drill plan |
| **Micro** | The individual level — everyday experience | A family does not have an emergency kit at home |

### Part B — The Five Whys

The Five Whys is a tool for finding the real cause of a problem.
You start with one problem and ask "Why?" five times.
Each answer becomes the next question.

**Step 3.** Choose one problem from your chart above.
Write it here:

> I am starting with this problem: ___________________________________________

**Step 4.** Fill in the Five Whys below.

**Here is an example:**

> **Problem:** People do not know what to do when an earthquake starts.
> Why? → Because they never practiced a drill.
> Why? → Because their school or workplace did not hold drills.
> Why? → Because there is no requirement to hold earthquake drills.
> Why? → Because local governments have not passed a law about it.
> Why? → Because earthquake safety is not seen as a priority in that community.

### Part C — Root Cause Categories

Every big problem has different types of causes.
Here are three types:

| Type | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | A broken or missing object or structure |
| **Human** | A person does not know something or has not been trained |
| **Organizational** | A rule, policy, or system is missing or not working |
