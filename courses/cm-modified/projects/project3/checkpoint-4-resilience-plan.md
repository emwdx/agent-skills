## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Part A — SMART Goal

A SMART goal is a goal that is:

| Letter | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **S** — Specific | It says exactly what you want to do |
| **M** — Measurable | You can count or measure whether it happened |
| **A** — Achievable | It is realistic for one person to do |
| **R** — Relevant | It connects to the problem you are solving |
| **T** — Time-bound | It has a deadline |

**Step 1.** Read the example SMART goal below.

> **Example:**
> I will share my earthquake safety quiz with 10 students at my school by the end of the semester so they can learn three basic earthquake safety rules.

**Step 2.** Fill in your own SMART goal using the frame:

> I will [what you will do] ___________________________________________
> with [who will use it or see it] ___________________________________________
> by [when] ___________________________________________
> so that [what will happen or change] ___________________________________________

---

### Part B — Minimum Viable Project (MVP)

An MVP is the simplest version of your project that could still help someone.
You do not need all the features. You just need the most important one.

**Step 3.** Read the example MVP description below.

> **Example:**
> The MVP of my earthquake quiz is just five questions with correct/incorrect feedback.
> I do not need sound, animations, or a score tracker.
> Just the questions and the answers are enough to teach someone something useful.

**Step 4.** Fill in your own MVP description using the frame:

> The most important part of my tool is: ___________________________________________
>
> A person could still learn something useful if the tool only had: ___________________________________________
>
> The parts I do not need right now are: ___________________________________________

---

### Part C — Plan for Resilience

Every project has things that could go wrong.
Planning ahead for problems is called resilience planning.

**Step 5.** Choose one problem from the list below that could happen with your tool.
Put a checkmark next to it.

- [ ] The website or app stops working
- [ ] People do not understand what the tool is asking them to do
- [ ] The information in the tool turns out to be wrong
- [ ] People do not want to use it
- [ ] My own concern: ___________________________________________

**Step 6.** Fill in the resilience plan frame:

> **The problem:** ___________________________________________
>
> **Why this could happen:** ___________________________________________
>
> **My plan to fix it:** ___________________________________________
>
> **One person who could help me fix it:** ___________________________________________

**Here is an example:**

> **The problem:** People do not understand what the quiz is asking.
>
> **Why this could happen:** The questions might use words that are too technical.
>
> **My plan to fix it:** I will ask one classmate to try the quiz and tell me which question confused them. Then I will rewrite that question in simpler words.
>
> **One person who could help me fix it:** My teacher or a classmate who has not seen the tool yet.

### Part D — Who This Helps

**Step 7.** Fill in this sentence frame to describe the impact of your tool.

> My tool could help [who] ___________________________________________
> by [doing what] ___________________________________________.
>
> One specific example of this is: ___________________________________________
>
> Another specific example is: ___________________________________________

**Here is an example:**

> My tool could help families in earthquake zones
> by teaching them what to do in the first 60 seconds of shaking.
>
> One specific example of this is: A parent could use the quiz to practice with their kids at home.
>
> Another specific example is: A teacher could show the checklist on a projector to review safety steps with the class.
