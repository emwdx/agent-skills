These are the modified proficiency scales for this student's adjusted curriculum track. They are anchored to the same five Changemakers competencies but adapted to his current skill level and learning needs. Use these scales — not the standard Changemakers scales — for any assessment, rubric, or checkpoint document generated for him.

The key difference from the standard scales: L3 for this student describes concrete, structured, supported demonstrations of competency. He is not expected to work independently from a blank page. He is expected to complete guided tasks with enough quality and specificity to show genuine understanding.

---

## Creativity

**L4** — Demonstrates in-depth synthesis and application of ideas with complexity or integration.
- Produces a product that reflects a novel and useful idea with exceptional originality and practicality.
- Shows evidence of multiple design cycles that explore possibilities and generate improvements based on stakeholder feedback.
- Synthesizes and combines multiple perspectives to explain how the product demonstrates deep understanding of the problem.

**L3** — Chooses a brainstorming activity and completes it as part of a project to generate ideas.
- Selects one idea and completes a guided template explaining what it is and how it helps solve the problem.
- Builds a simple prototype (physical or digital) using a step-by-step checklist.
- Uses a feedback form to get one suggestion and completes the sentence: "One way I could improve this is…"

**L2** — Uses a provided brainstorming sheet to generate at least three different ideas.
- Identifies which of three sample ideas is most useful and explains why using sentence starters ("This one is best because…").
- Completes a simple idea planner: What is the problem? What do people need? What is one way to help?

## Critical Thinking (Adaptive Thinking)

**L3** — Selects one claim from three given options and matches it to supporting evidence snippets from a given source.
- Completes sentence stems to describe one limitation of the evidence.
- Writes or explains a recommendation with two specific reasons from a source.

**L2** — Matches key terms (claim, evidence, source, credibility) to their definitions.
- Identifies whether provided statements are fact or opinion.
- Sorts three short articles into "Strong source / Weak source" using an annotated CRAAP checklist.
- Writes a sentence that represents a summary of a written paragraph.

---

## Collaboration

**L3** — Fills a simple log sheet showing the task for the day, a checkbox for completion, and a sentence frame showing how he knows it has been completed.
- Gives one piece of written or video feedback to a peer using a provided praise-plus-suggestion sentence frame.
- Uses a role-choice menu to pick a participation role.
- States a personal goal on a sentence-stem card ("When we meet/work, I will … to help my learning.").

**L2** — Identifies whether specific behavior is following a norm of collaboration or not.
- Matches norms of collaboration to a definition.
- Uses a sentence stem to resolve a specific problem scenario in collaboration.

---

## Communication

**L4** — Adapts communication tools and strategies to meet the needs of diverse audiences.
- Applies a sophisticated level of craftsmanship.
- Extends quality and depth by following, adapting, or breaking conventions of a medium or style.

**L3** — Produces a small-group presentation (3-4 peers and teacher) with 3 slides max, following a provided outline (hook, main idea, call-to-action).
- Checklist includes: readable font, images labelled, grammar and spell-check done.
- Brief Q&A using a question card bank (student picks one card).
- Follows steps in a tutorial to produce a piece of media.

**L2** — Identifies how media conventions appear in an example.
- Fills blanks in a model outline for communicating a message (hook ___, main idea ___).
- Identifies which of three exemplars best communicates a message in a particular medium and explains why.

---

## Advocacy and Action

**L4** — Creates a plan for involvement in a change project.
- Takes feedback from a stakeholder and uses it to revise a plan for change.
- Presents revised version in a new format (written, audio, or poster).

**L3** — Uses sentence stems to summarize key points from a stakeholder empathy source (recorded or scripted).
- Chooses a proposed action from a choice board and completes a guided Action Plan template: What is the issue? Who will this help? What will I do? When?
- Describes (verbally, in writing, or via video) how this action might help someone in their community, using at least two specific examples.
- Follows a checklist to complete a small action.

**L2** — Matches words (stakeholder, empathy, issue, impact) to pictures or definitions.
- Identifies two people or groups affected by a specific issue, using sentence stems or a picture prompt.
- Sorts sample actions into "helps with the issue" or "doesn't help."
- Uses a sentence stem to write one short idea about how to help solve a problem ("To help with ____, I could ____.").

## How to use these scales when writing checkpoints

Write the assessment task first, then check it against the relevant scale level. The task should make the scale criteria visible without requiring the student to interpret what proficiency looks like.

For L3 checkpoints: the task should include the sentence frame, template, checklist, or choice structure described in the scale. The student demonstrates proficiency by completing the structure with genuine content — not by creating the structure himself.

For L2 checkpoints: the task should be matching, sorting, identifying, or fill-in-the-blank. The cognitive demand is real; the production demand is low.

Never write an open-ended prompt and label it L3. If there is no frame, template, or checklist, it is not adapted to his needs.
