# Philosophy

## Vision
Most AI-in-education conversations focus on prompting skills — how to ask an AI to make a lesson plan or generate a rubric. This misses the prior problem entirely.
AI agents default to the shortest path. In education, the shortest path produces generic lesson plans, rubrics filled with abstract buzzwords, and materials that could belong to any course at any school. The problem is not the AI. The problem is that nobody has done the work of making a team's knowledge legible enough to encode.
This repository is built on a different premise. The value is not in the AI knowing how to design curriculum — it already knows enough to produce something passable. The value is in encoding the process discipline, values, and decision-making strategies of experienced educators so that the AI works at their level, not at the level of a generic substitute.
The skill files in this repo are the artifact. The process of making them is the real work.
## The Problem with AI in Education
When a skilled teacher designs a learning experience, they are drawing on years of accumulated judgment — about how students in a particular context think, what they resist, where they rush, what makes them care. That knowledge is almost never written down. It lives in one person's head, or in the collective memory of a small team, and it disappears when people leave.
This is the same problem schools have always had with onboarding. A new co-teacher gets told "you'll pick it up as you go." A course that took years to develop gets handed over as a folder of slide decks. The institutional knowledge — the values, the style, the reasoning behind decisions — never transfers cleanly.
AI makes this problem more visible because AI has none of that context by default. Ask it to generate an activity for a course it knows nothing about and it will produce something technically competent and entirely wrong for the room.
The solution is not better prompting. It is doing the upstream work of making implicit knowledge explicit.
## The Process
The process that produced this framework looks like this:
Sit with a team. Do the empathy work — observe, interview, collect existing documents, understand how they make decisions and what they care about. Synthesize that into a set of skill files. Present them back not as deliverables but as a mirror. Let the team revise them. Now they have something that works for AI sessions, works as onboarding documentation, and works as a values clarification exercise for the team itself.
The readback moment matters. Most teams have never had someone reflect back to them what they actually value and how they actually make decisions, in a form they can read and argue with. That conversation alone changes how a team understands itself.
The skill files are the output. The process of building them — the interviews, the analysis, the synthesis, the readback — is the offering.
This is organizational knowledge capture. It has always been essential work. Nobody in education has treated it that way. This repository is an attempt to change that.
